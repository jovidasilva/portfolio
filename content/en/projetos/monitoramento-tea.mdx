*Undergraduate research (PIBIC 2025/2026) at Universidade Ceuma, on a research scholarship, advised by Prof. Jonathan Araujo Queiroz.*

Autistic people can have anxiety crises and sensory overload that start with almost no warning. The research proposes a low-cost wearable that measures signals like heart rate, temperature and skin conductance, and an AI that recognizes in those signals when a crisis is building up, so family and caregivers can act sooner.

## What I did

My advisor's project is the device. My work plan was the AI software that analyzes its data.

Real clinical data is hard to get, mainly for ethical reasons, so I worked with public physiological signal datasets and simulated data. In Python, I built the signal processing: normalization, noise filters, class balancing and splitting into time windows. For the models, I chose a convolutional network (CNN) to find patterns in the signals and an LSTM to follow how they change over time, comparing both against SVM and Random Forest.

## How far it got

The data processing is done and the architecture proved feasible, but the models haven't been validated yet. They still need testing on real data and fitting onto low-power hardware, since the goal is to run them on the device itself.

The research became the paper [Dispositivo eletrônico reconfigurável baseado em inteligência artificial para suporte à saúde de indivíduos com transtorno do espectro autista](https://www.even3.com.br/anais/iii-seminario-internacional-educacao-empreendedorismo-gestao-projetos/1423208/) (in Portuguese), presented at the III International Seminar on Education, Entrepreneurship and Project Management (2025), and an extended abstract at Universidade Ceuma's IV SEMICTI (2026).
